## What the license protects, and its limits

The restrictions cover protectable project code, scripts, documentation, and
original assets that the licensor has authority to license. They cover copied
portions and derivatives as well as whole-app copies. The [brand policy](BRANDING.md)
addresses impersonation, confusing listings, and misuse of project identity.

Copyright does not give the project a monopoly on audio workflows, product ideas,
functionality, programming techniques, or independently written implementations.
A restrictive license also does not establish trademark registration, guarantee
exclusive rights in a descriptive name, or prove ownership of an image.

The app does not acquire ownership of your audio, recordings, settings, or
library. You still need any permissions required for the content you use.

## Older releases and repository forks

Use the license supplied with the exact tag, commit, or downloaded artifact.
Changing the current branch does not withdraw an earlier grant for an earlier
copy. For example, a copy supplied under PolyForm Noncommercial keeps those
terms, which allow certain noncommercial changes and distribution. New additions
must be assessed under the terms that cover those additions.

Public GitHub repositories also carry rights under GitHub's applicable terms.
The project license does not cancel those rights. A platform fork is not, by
itself, permission to sell, rebrand, or distribute an application outside the
scope of the applicable grants.

## Third-party software and artwork

Read the [third-party overview](../THIRDPARTY_LICENSES.md) and
[generated Rust notices](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.html). Those licenses control the
identified components independently of the project license.

- **MPL:** covered source files retain the MPL and its source-availability rules,
  including when unmodified components are distributed in executable form.
- **LGPL:** required user modification, library replacement, relinking, and
  debugging/reverse-engineering rights remain available.
- **Permissive licenses:** required upstream copyright, license, and attribution
  notices still accompany the material.
- **AppStream metadata:** its separate CC0 declaration remains in effect.

Bundled libraries require release-specific compliance work. A generic upstream
homepage or a generated Rust notice file alone is not proof that a binary release
satisfies every native-library source or relinking obligation.

## Contributions and written exceptions

Contributors retain ownership and grant the rights described in
[CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md). Agreement is recorded in the pull request
before acceptance. Third-party material must be identified separately; a
contributor cannot grant rights they do not possess.

Commercial, redistribution, modification, and branding permissions must identify
their scope in writing. Donations, purchases without such terms, and unanswered
requests are not blanket permission.
